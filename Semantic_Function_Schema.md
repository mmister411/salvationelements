## **Semantic Function Schema: Updated Documentation**

The **Semantic Function Schema** is a structured analytical tool for examining spiritual, ethical, conceptual, or operational elements. It maps meaning, function, causality, evidence, scope, and distortion. It is not a summary or topical overview. It is a logic-based, condition-sensitive diagnostic form that defines what something is called, what it does, how it works, and under what conditions it operates.

---

### **Element Title**

A functional, concrete descriptor of a specific attribute, relationship, or event. Avoid general terms. The title should describe the actual mechanism or action under review. If generalization is unavoidable, the concept likely needs to be divided and analyzed through separate schema entries.

---

### **1. Terminology**

**A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By**
Lists synonymous terms, phrases, or conceptually equivalent expressions across theological, philosophical, or practical domains. Enables semantic mapping across contexts while preserving core meaning. Ensures cross-context recognition without redefining the element.

---

### **2. Causality**

**Source or Cause of Event, Operation, or Methodology**
Identifies the origin, agent, mechanism, or process that produces or initiates the described function. May include divine actions, doctrinal systems, natural/spiritual laws, human responses, or situational triggers. This section clarifies how the function comes into effect. Some elements may have multiple legitimate causes.

---

### **3. Validation**

**Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place**

* **Affirmation:**
  Lists the positive evidences or qualifying conditions confirming that the function has occurred. Should be traceable to authoritative sources and logically or observationally coherent.

* **Negation:**
  Identifies conditions that nullify, invalidate, or contradict the function. Includes disqualifying states, actions, or counterconditions that block or deny authenticity.

---

### **4. Effects**

**Accounts For**
Describes the results, consequences, or conditions made possible or resolved by the function. Outlines the functional utility or role the element plays in broader systems. Shows downstream impact, explanatory reach, or transformation resulting from the element’s operation.

---

### **5. Considerations**

* **Exemptions:**
  Identifies subjects or conditions not bound by the function. These may be based on maturity, status, context, or special precedent. Exemptions clarify when standard application does not apply.

* **Limitations:**
  Defines the boundaries or constraints of the function. Describes what it cannot do, when it ceases to apply, or where it is only partially effective. Prevents category drift and overextension.

* **Inferences:**
  Deductions logically or textually derived from the element’s nature or structure. Establishes secondary insights, implications, or consequences that follow from its stated function without speculative leap.

* **Alerts:**
  Highlights typical risks, misapplications, or spiritual hazards tied to misunderstanding or misusing the function.

* **Corruptions:**
  Identifies mimicries or distortions that replace or counterfeit the authentic function. Includes theological errors, conceptual substitutes, and structural misrepresentations that retain surface language while altering substance.
