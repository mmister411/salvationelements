Scriptual Christology
======================

# Introduction

This document aims to provide an in-depth, defensible understanding, part-by-part, of what, who, and how the scriptures present the Lord Jesus. The Lord Jesus is the sum total mystery of Scripture. Jesus is the fulcrum on which all creation pivots. It is no easy task to seek to gather together all scriptural evidence for the multiple roles and purposes that Jesus fulfills. However, it is both true that He is worthy of all service and worship and it is His divine desire that you may come to know Him as He is. Each section of this work will delve into a major area of the operations or attributes of Jesus to provide synonymous terms, truth implications, and scriptural references at every turn. May the Holy Spirit guide you to use these tools to better know the Lord Jesus as He is written.

#### Hermeneutical Disclaimer:

This analysis proceeds on the methodological assumption that theology must arise traceably from exegesis of the text (Acts 17:11; 2 Tim 2:15). All readings and conclusions must respect immediate literary and historical context while additionally being apt to consider wider material from the given book and canon as applicable (Luke 24:27; Acts 28:23). Systematic theology, interpretive tradition, naming conventions, science, archaeology, and philosophy possess intrinsic value as disciplines (Rom 1:20; Prov 25:2). Engagement with these disciplines must not introduce meanings that are unreasonably foreign to the Word’s original context (Matt 15:9; Col 2:8), go beyond what the text cogently supports (1 Cor 4:6), or contradict what the text explicitly states (Deut 4:2; Prov 30:6; Rev 22:18–19). Interpretive lenses are necessarily embedded in any interpretive operation of scripture but must not weigh in at the expense of the text’s self-defined logos, ethos, or pathos (2 Cor 4:2; Ps 119:130; Heb 4:12). If confusion persists, I seek the Lord in prayer, that by His Spirit I may gain understanding and discernment (Jas 1:5; John 16:13; Prov 3:5–6).

## Scriptural Christology in Functional Terms

- Uncreated Divinity, Very God from Very God
  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Establish the full deity of Jesus Christ as uncreated, co-eternal, and consubstantial with the Father and Spirit. Some key texts: John 1:1–3, Colossians 1:15–20, Hebrews 1:3–6, Philippians 2:5–11, Matthew 28:19.
  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:  
    - YAHWEH rained down brimstone from YAHWEH (Genesis 19:24; Genesis 1:26; Genesis 3:22; Genesis 11:7; Isaiah 6:8; Hosea 1:7; Zechariah 2:9–11; Malachi 3:1; Psalm 110:1; Daniel 7:13–14; Matthew 3:16–17; John 1:1; John 17:5)
    - In Him dwells all the fullness of Deity bodily (Colossians 2:9; Colossians 1:19; John 1:14; John 1:18; Hebrews 1:3; 2 Corinthians 4:4; Philippians 2:6; John 14:9–10; Isaiah 9:6; Revelation 21:22)
    - Before Abraham was, I AM (John 8:58; Exodus 3:14; Isaiah 43:10–13; John 4:26; John 6:20; John 8:24; John 8:28; John 13:19; John 18:5–6; Isaiah 41:4; Isaiah 46:4; Isaiah 48:12)
    - The Word was God (John 1:1; John 1:18; John 20:28; Romans 9:5; Titus 2:13; Hebrews 1:8; 2 Peter 1:1; 1 John 5:20; Revelation 19:13))

  - Source, Cause or Warrant (Why we conclude Jesus is full Deity one with Father & Spirit)
    - Jesus is called God directly by inspired authors (John 1:1; John 20:28; Romans 9:5; Titus 2:13; Hebrews 1:8).
    - Jesus bears names and titles that only YHWH bears: “I AM,” “First and Last,” “Immanuel” (John 8:58; Isaiah 44:6; Matthew 1:23).
    - Jesus possesses divine attributes: eternal preexistence, omniscience, omnipresence, and sovereignty (John 1:1; Matthew 28:20; Revelation 2:23).
    - Jesus performs works exclusive to God: creation, sustaining the cosmos, forgiving sin, and granting life (Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 1:3; Mark 2:7; John 10:28).
    - Jesus shares glory with the Father that predates creation (John 17:5).
    - Jesus is worshiped rightly by angels and humans, without correction or rebuke (Matthew 28:9; Hebrews 1:6; Revelation 5:13–14).
    - Jesus is one with the Father and the Spirit in being, operation, and name (John 10:30; Matthew 28:19; Colossians 2:9).
    - Jesus is the visible image and exact imprint of the invisible God (Colossians 1:15; Hebrews 1:3; John 14:9).
    - Jesus fulfills the identity and function of the Angel of YHWH and the Word of YHWH in the Old Testament, who speaks with divine authority and receives divine honor (Genesis 16; Exodus 3; Zechariah 2:9–11).

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:
    - Affirmation (Separate or related Evidentiary Assertions or prerequisites of validity):
      - The Father explicitly calls the Son “God” (Hebrews 1:8).
      - Jesus forgives sin directly (Mark 2:5–7), a divine prerogative (Isaiah 43:25).
      - Jesus receives worship without rebuke (Matthew 28:9,17; John 20:28; Revelation 5:8–14).
      - Jesus has power over life and judgment (John 5:21–27).
      - The divine Name is placed upon Jesus (Philippians 2:9–11; Isaiah 45:23).
      - Jesus is worshiped by all creation post-resurrection without rebuke (Matthew 28:17; Revelation 5:13).
      - Jesus is addressed with divine language and titles in resurrected encounters (John 20:28; Revelation 1:17–18).
      - Jesus reigns from heaven at the right hand of the Father in shared authority (Acts 7:56; Hebrews 1:3; Philippians 2:9–11).
      - Jesus’ words are treated as absolute, final, and unbreakable (Matthew 24:35; John 12:48).
      - Jesus imparts the Spirit with sovereign prerogative (John 20:22; Acts 2:33).
      - Jesus exercises judgment and grants eternal life (John 5:21–27; Revelation 20:11–15).
      - Jesus is the object of saving faith and prayer (Acts 7:59; John 14:1, 14).
      - Jesus’ name is confessed in salvation and in universal acknowledgment (Philippians 2:10–11; Isaiah 45:23).

    - Negation or Rebuttal (Counter conditionals undermining validity or rebuttals to would-be counters):
      - If Jesus were created, He could not be eternal (Micah 5:2; John 1:3).
      - If Jesus were not God, He could not be worshiped without blasphemy (Exodus 34:14; Matthew 4:10).
      - If He were only a prophet, He would not claim equality with the Father (John 10:30–33).
      - If He were merely a representative, He could not bear the divine Name and receive the divine glory (Isaiah 42:8; John 17:5).
      - Jesus cannot be a created being because all things were made through Him (John 1:3; Colossians 1:16).
      - Jesus cannot be merely a creature because He receives worship that is forbidden to give to any created being (Matthew 28:9; Revelation 5:13–14; Exodus 34:14).
      - Jesus cannot be only a prophet or man because He uses the divine name “I AM” in reference to Himself (John 8:58; Exodus 3:14).
      - Jesus cannot be less than God because He claims that seeing Him is equivalent to seeing the Father (John 14:9).
      - Jesus cannot be other than divine because He forgives sin as an intrinsic authority, not by delegated permission (Mark 2:5–7).
      - Jesus cannot be a glorified creature because He shared the Father’s eternal glory before creation (John 17:5; Isaiah 42:8).
      - Jesus cannot be merely a teacher because He speaks with divine authority over the Law and the Temple (Matthew 5:21–22; John 2:16).
      - Jesus cannot be an idol because invoking His name brings salvation as only the Lord's name does (Acts 4:12; Romans 10:13).
      - Jesus cannot be a false claimant because His resurrection vindicates His divine Sonship and rule (Romans 1:4; Acts 2:36).

  
  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Authority and efficacy of Jesus' redemptive work.
    - Sufficiency of Jesus as the mediator between God and man (1 Timothy 2:5).
    - Weight behind the eternal covenantal promises fulfilled in Christ (2 Corinthians 1:20).
    - Affirms the possibility of union with God through Christ (John 14:20–23; Colossians 1:27).
    - Resolves how God Himself bears sin and overcomes death (Acts 20:28; 1 Corinthians 15:54–57).
  
  - Considerations:  
    - Exemptions (Circumstances where Element would otherwise function but not applicable):
      - Not applicable. Jesus has always been and will always be Jesus.
    - Limitations (Boundaries of the Element's designed validity, effect activation or inclusion):
      - His being fully human has been presented as the reasoning for the words "Only the Father knows the day or the hour" but this is a thin conclusion for this biblical mystery ()
    - Inferences (Logically derived explications, implications, deductions or inductions):  
      - Jesus’ words bear divine authority and cannot be relativized or subordinated to other messengers (Matthew 24:35; Hebrews 2:1–3).
      - Worship of Jesus is necessary, not optional (John 5:23; Revelation 5:12–14).
      - Christ’s presence transcends time and location (Matthew 18:20; Matthew 28:20).
      - The divine essence is shared, not divided, among Father, Son, and Spirit (Matthew 28:19; John 14:16–17, 23).

    - Alerts (Warnings, Admonitions, Pitfalls):  
      - Beware of reducing Jesus to merely a moral teacher, prophet, or created being (Colossians 2:8).
      - Avoid modalism: the Father, Son, and Spirit are not merely different forms of the same person (John 14:26; Matthew 3:16–17).
      - Reject attempts to pit Jesus’ humanity against His deity; both are true, not alternating or fragmentary (1 John 4:2–3; John 1:14).
      - Denying the full divinity of Christ is to deny the gospel (2 John 1:7; John 8:24).
  
    - Corruptions (distortions that mimic, replace, or misrepresent the element):  
      - Arianism: claims Jesus was a created being, not eternal God.
      - Adoptionism: teaches Jesus was a mere man adopted by God.
      - Modalism/Sabellianism: fails to distinguish the persons of the Trinity.
      - Docetism: denies the true humanity of Jesus while overstating a spiritual-only nature.
      - New Age distortions: reduce Jesus to a spiritual exemplar or enlightened guru.
      - Modern liberal theology: strips Jesus of supernatural identity and treats His divinity as metaphorical.

2. Word of the Lord becomes Lord Prophet, Son of David
  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Identify the second divine person as the "Word of the LORD" and "Angel of the LORD" who speaks and appears in the Old Testament with divine prerogatives and first-person covenantal authority.
    - Show continuity from this revealed figure to Jesus, who fulfills the role of the promised Prophet like Moses and the Davidic Messiah, whom David calls “Lord.”
    - Establish that this figure is both distinct from and yet one with the LORD, affirming plurality within the Godhead and divine self-disclosure in the economy of revelation.
    - Key texts: Genesis 16:7–13; Exodus 3:2–6; 1 Samuel 3:1–21; Psalm 110:1; Isaiah 6:1–10 cf. John 12:41; Zechariah 2:8–11; Matthew 22:41–46; Acts 3:22–23.

  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:
    - The Word of the LORD (Genesis 15:1–6; 1 Samuel 3:21; Jeremiah 1:4)
    - The Angel of the LORD (Genesis 16:7–13; Exodus 3:2–6; Judges 13:3–22)
    - YHWH speaking from visible presence (Exodus 33:9–11; Numbers 12:6–8)
    - The Lord who sends the Lord (Zechariah 2:9–11; Malachi 3:1)
    - The one seated at the right hand (Psalm 110:1; Matthew 22:44)
    - The Prophet like Moses (Deuteronomy 18:15–19; Acts 3:22)
    - Son of David and David’s Lord (2 Samuel 7:12–14; Matthew 22:42–46)

  - Source, Cause or Warrant for Event, Operation, Methodology or having Attribute 
    - The Angel of the LORD speaks as God, identifies as God, and is worshiped, yet is distinguished from the LORD (Genesis 16:13; Exodus 3:2–6).
    - The Word of the LORD appears as a visible person and speaks in first-person divine authority (1 Samuel 3:1–10; Jeremiah 1:4–10).
    - The LORD says to "my Lord" in Psalm 110:1, showing an intra-YHWH relationship.
    - Jesus applies this passage to Himself, demanding a conclusion that David’s Son is also David’s Lord (Matthew 22:41–46).
    - John 12:41 confirms that Isaiah saw Jesus’ glory in Isaiah 6, identifying the enthroned Lord as the preincarnate Christ.

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:  
    - Affirmation:
      - Jesus identifies Himself as the one sent from the Father, speaking the Father's words (John 8:26–28; John 12:49–50).
      - Jesus fulfills the pattern of the Prophet like Moses (Deuteronomy 18:15; Acts 3:22–23).
      - Jesus is called “the Son of David” by the crowds and does not deny it (Matthew 21:9; 22:41–46).
      - Jesus teaches with absolute authority, unlike the scribes (Mark 1:22).
      - The people declare He is the Prophet (John 6:14), and He speaks what He hears from the Father (John 14:10).
    
    - Negation or Rebuttal:
      - If Jesus were not the divine Word, then His claim to have seen Abraham and to be before him (John 8:58) would be false.
      - If Jesus were not the Lord in Psalm 110, He could not be both David’s son and superior.
      - If the Angel of the LORD were merely an angel, He could not accept worship or speak in the first person as YHWH.
      - If Jesus were not the fulfillment of the Prophet like Moses, then His authority would be derivative and not final.
      - If Jesus were not divine, the glory Isaiah saw could not have been His (John 12:41).

  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Explains Jesus’ prophetic authority as rooted in preincarnate divine activity.
    - Grounds Jesus’ role in redemptive revelation, speaking directly for God as God.
    - Affirms divine continuity between Old and New Testaments.
    - Reveals the inner-personal dynamics of the Godhead in progressive revelation.
    - Supports Jesus’ messianic claim as both the heir to David’s throne and the eternal Word.

  - Considerations:  
    - Exemptions:
      - Jesus did not appear in every theophany or prophetic utterance; some visions and voices are from the Father or the Spirit (Daniel 7:9–10).
    
    - Limitations:
      - Prophetic office includes divine speech but does not reduce Christ’s role to mere messenger (Hebrews 1:1–3).
      - The term “Angel” refers to function, not nature; it does not imply creaturehood.

    - Inferences:
      - The plurality seen in Old Testament passages (Psalm 110; Zechariah 2) prepares for Trinitarian theology.
      - Jesus’ authority in teaching, healing, and judgment is grounded in His identity as the speaking Lord.
      - The coherence of scriptural revelation requires divine continuity of the speaker.

    - Alerts:
      - Do not confuse “angel” (messenger) with ontological angelic nature.
      - Avoid reducing Jesus to a moral reformer or sage detached from covenantal continuity.
      - Misreading “Son of David” solely as political title misses the divine element inherent in Psalm 110.

    - Corruptions:
      - Ebionism: denies the preexistence of Christ and His divine speech before incarnation.
      - Islam: recognizes Jesus as prophet but denies His divinity and eternal Word identity.
      - Modern critical theology: detaches Old Testament theophanies from Christological fulfillment.
      - Jehovah’s Witness theology: denies the full deity of the preincarnate Word.

3. The Restorative Seed of His Own Creation

  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Trace the promise of a Redeemer from Genesis 3:15 through prophetic expectation to fulfillment in the incarnation.
    - Show that Jesus is the promised Seed who would crush the serpent, born of woman, yet eternal and divine.
    - Establish that Jesus entered creation not as a visitor but as the Creator made flesh to redeem it from within.
    - Identify Jesus as the Immanuel born of a virgin, the Servant of the LORD who suffers to redeem, and the Son of Man given dominion.
    - Key texts: Genesis 3:15; Isaiah 7:14; Isaiah 9:6; Isaiah 42:1–9; Isaiah 53; Daniel 7:13–14; Luke 1:26–35; John 1:14.

  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:
    - The Seed of the Woman (Genesis 3:15)
    - Immanuel – “God with us” (Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:23)
    - Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace (Isaiah 9:6)
    - The Servant of the LORD (Isaiah 42; 49; 50; 52–53)
    - The Branch from Jesse (Isaiah 11:1–5; Zechariah 3:8; 6:12)
    - The Son of Man coming with the clouds (Daniel 7:13–14)
    - The Word became flesh (John 1:14)

  - Source, Cause or Warrant for Event, Operation, Methodology or having Attribute 
    - God promised a Seed to crush the serpent’s head (Genesis 3:15).
    - The virgin birth fulfills the sign of Immanuel (Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:20–23).
    - The eternal Son was given to us as a child born (Isaiah 9:6).
    - Jesus is conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of Mary, taking human nature (Luke 1:31–35; Philippians 2:7).
    - The Word became flesh and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth (John 1:14).
    - The Servant is called from the womb to restore Israel and be a light to the nations (Isaiah 49:1–6).
    - The Son of Man receives dominion and glory in heaven after coming in likeness of men (Daniel 7:13–14; Philippians 2:8–11).

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:  
    - Affirmation:
      - The genealogies trace Jesus through David and Abraham, affirming His Messianic lineage (Matthew 1; Luke 3).
      - Mary’s conception by the Holy Spirit confirms divine origin and virgin fulfillment (Luke 1:34–35).
      - Angels announce the birth of the Savior who is Christ the Lord (Luke 2:11).
      - Simeon and Anna testify that He is the promised salvation for Israel and the Gentiles (Luke 2:25–32).
      - Jesus refers to Himself as the Son of Man and connects His mission to the prophetic Servant (Luke 19:10; Mark 10:45).
    
    - Negation or Rebuttal:
      - If Jesus were not born of a woman, He could not fulfill Genesis 3:15 or identify with mankind (Hebrews 2:14).
      - If Jesus were merely human, He could not be Immanuel—God with us (Isaiah 7:14; Matthew 1:23).
      - If He were not the Servant, then Isaiah 53 remains unfulfilled.
      - If Jesus were not both human and divine, He could not mediate between God and man (1 Timothy 2:5).
      - If Jesus were not the Son of Man given dominion, He would not have universal and eternal kingship (Daniel 7:14).

  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Makes possible substitutionary atonement through a true human nature.
    - Fulfills the promised victory over sin, death, and Satan from Genesis 3.
    - Enables redemption from within creation, not from outside it.
    - Brings God's eternal plan to fullness in time and flesh.
    - Reveals the personal, intimate nearness of God with His people.

  - Considerations:  
    - Exemptions:
      - None; the incarnation is essential and non-repeatable. Jesus must be born of woman and fully enter humanity.
    
    - Limitations:
      - The incarnation does not entail the loss of divine nature; Jesus remains fully God.
      - The taking on of flesh is limited to a unique, once-for-all historical act.

    - Inferences:
      - God’s plan of redemption was embedded in creation from the beginning.
      - Jesus as “Immanuel” affirms God’s intention to dwell with humanity permanently (Revelation 21:3).
      - The incarnation confirms God’s justice (punishment of sin) and mercy (bearing it Himself).
      - The incarnation necessitates that the Redeemer be both Creator and creature.

    - Alerts:
      - Avoid treating the incarnation as temporary or reversible.
      - Do not separate Jesus’ humanity from His divinity—He is not two persons but one Person with two natures.
      - Be cautious of theological systems that deny or minimize the virgin birth.
      - Avoid reducing the incarnation to metaphor or moral example.

    - Corruptions:
      - Docetism: denies Jesus’ true humanity.
      - Adoptionism: treats Jesus as a man elevated to divine status.
      - Nestorianism: improperly separates Christ's two natures into two persons.
      - Unitarianism: denies divine preexistence and virgin birth.
      - Liberal theology: reinterprets the virgin birth symbolically or mythologically.

4. Unspotted Lamb slain from the foundation of the cosmos as an Atoning Redemptive Sacrifice for Mankind from the first to the Last

  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Present Jesus as the sinless Lamb of God whose death fulfills the entire sacrificial system and embodies the eternal redemptive plan.
    - Emphasize that His death was voluntary, substitutionary, and sufficient to atone for sin from the beginning of human history to its end.
    - Show that His resurrection confirms the acceptance of the sacrifice and secures life for all who believe.
    - Key texts: Exodus 12; Leviticus 16; Isaiah 53; John 1:29; Romans 3:21–26; 1 Peter 1:19–21; Revelation 13:8.

  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:
    - The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29)
    - The Passover Lamb (Exodus 12; 1 Corinthians 5:7)
    - The Sin Offering (Leviticus 16; 2 Corinthians 5:21)
    - The Suffering Servant (Isaiah 53)
    - The Lamb slain from the foundation of the world (Revelation 13:8)
    - The once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 10:12–14)
    - The ransom for many (Mark 10:45)
    - The blood of the covenant (Matthew 26:28)

  - Source, Cause or Warrant for Event, Operation, Methodology or having Attribute
    - God instituted blood sacrifice as the means of atonement (Leviticus 17:11).
    - Jesus, though sinless, bore our sins as a substitute (Isaiah 53:4–6; 1 Peter 2:24).
    - His sacrifice was foreordained before the foundation of the world (1 Peter 1:20; Revelation 13:8).
    - Jesus offered Himself voluntarily in obedience to the Father’s will (John 10:17–18; Hebrews 9:14).
    - God set Him forth as a propitiation by His blood to demonstrate righteousness (Romans 3:25–26).
    - His resurrection declares Him vindicated and alive forever (Romans 4:25; Revelation 1:18).

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:  
    - Affirmation:
      - Jesus is declared innocent by Pilate, and sinless by those closest to Him (Luke 23:4; Hebrews 4:15; 1 Peter 2:22).
      - Jesus is recognized as the Lamb of God before His passion (John 1:29).
      - His death coincides with Passover, aligning with the type (John 19:14–36; Exodus 12).
      - The temple veil was torn at His death, indicating access to God opened (Matthew 27:51; Hebrews 10:19–20).
      - His blood is the basis for New Covenant forgiveness (Matthew 26:28; Hebrews 9:15).
      - He rose on the third day, affirming divine acceptance of the offering (1 Corinthians 15:3–4).

    - Negation or Rebuttal:
      - If Jesus had sinned, He could not bear others’ sin (Hebrews 7:26–27).
      - If He were merely martyred, His death would lack atoning power (John 10:18).
      - If there were other sufficient sacrifices, His would not be final or necessary (Hebrews 10:1–4).
      - If Christ were not raised, His death would be void and faith futile (1 Corinthians 15:17).
      - If Jesus were not appointed by God for this task, then His death would not fulfill righteousness (Romans 3:26).

  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Provides full and final atonement for sin (Hebrews 9:12; 10:14).
    - Reconciles sinners to God (2 Corinthians 5:18–19).
    - Satisfies divine justice while extending mercy (Romans 3:25–26).
    - Establishes the New Covenant in His blood (Luke 22:20).
    - Frees believers from condemnation and the power of death (Romans 8:1–3; Hebrews 2:14–15).
    - Secures access to the Father through His priestly intercession (Hebrews 4:14–16; 10:19–22).

  - Considerations:  
    - Exemptions:
      - No human, angel, or creature could serve as substitute; only the God-man qualifies (Hebrews 2:17; Acts 4:12).

    - Limitations:
      - The benefits of the atonement apply only to those united to Christ by faith (Romans 3:26; John 3:18).
      - His sacrificial work is unrepeatable and cannot be supplemented (Hebrews 10:12–14).

    - Inferences:
      - God’s plan of redemption was not reactive but eternal (Ephesians 1:4–7; 1 Peter 1:20).
      - The sacrificial system was a shadow pointing forward to Christ (Hebrews 9:23–24).
      - Forgiveness is not arbitrary but grounded in the costliness of substitution.

    - Alerts:
      - Do not confuse His death with mere moral influence or tragic martyrdom.
      - Avoid teaching universal atonement without faith-based reception (John 3:18).
      - Refrain from analogizing Christ’s death to pagan or mystical sacrifice; its roots are covenantal and revealed.
      - Avoid separating atonement from resurrection; the work is incomplete without vindication.

    - Corruptions:
      - Moral influence theory: reduces the cross to a symbol of love without substitution.
      - Universalism: assumes all are automatically saved regardless of belief.
      - Roman Catholic re-sacrifice: undermines the finality of Christ’s once-for-all offering (Hebrews 10:12–14).
      - Gnostic views: deny the need for a bodily death or resurrection.
      - Islamic view: denies that Jesus died at all, eliminating atonement.

5. Immortal Advocating High Priest of the New Covenant Church after the Order of Melchizedek

  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Explain the ongoing priesthood of Jesus Christ as the fulfillment of Psalm 110:4, distinct from the Levitical order.
    - Present Him as the mediator of the New Covenant, whose priesthood is unending, effective, and grounded in His own perfect, once-for-all sacrifice.
    - Emphasize His active intercession and role in securing the believer’s continual access to God.
    - Key texts: Psalm 110:4; Jeremiah 31:31–34; Luke 22:20; Hebrews 4–10.

  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:
    - High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 5:6)
    - Mediator of a better covenant (Hebrews 8:6)
    - Advocate with the Father (1 John 2:1)
    - One who ever lives to intercede (Hebrews 7:25)
    - Minister in the heavenly sanctuary (Hebrews 8:1–2)
    - The guarantor of the New Covenant (Hebrews 7:22)
    - The one who entered the Most Holy Place by His own blood (Hebrews 9:11–12)

  - Source, Cause or Warrant for Event, Operation, Methodology or having Attribute
    - God Himself swore and appointed Jesus a priest forever (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 5:5–6).
    - Jesus' priesthood is not by genealogy but by the power of an indestructible life (Hebrews 7:16).
    - His offering of Himself was perfect, needing no repetition (Hebrews 9:26–28).
    - He mediates the New Covenant established by His blood (Luke 22:20; Hebrews 8:6).
    - The Old Covenant was insufficient; a better covenant was required (Jeremiah 31:31–34; Hebrews 8:7–13).
    - His resurrection and ascension enable Him to serve forever (Hebrews 7:24; Romans 8:34).

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:
    - Affirmation:
      - Jesus is seated at the right hand of God, having completed His offering (Hebrews 10:12).
      - He lives forever and holds His priesthood permanently (Hebrews 7:24).
      - He intercedes continually for those who draw near to God through Him (Hebrews 7:25).
      - The veil was torn, symbolizing open access through His body (Hebrews 10:19–20).
      - Jesus did not enter an earthly tabernacle but heaven itself (Hebrews 9:24).
      - He is called the “Mediator of a new covenant” (Hebrews 9:15).

    - Negation or Rebuttal:
      - If Jesus were not risen, He could not function as a living priest (1 Corinthians 15:17; Hebrews 7:25).
      - If He were of the tribe of Levi, He could not qualify under the Melchizedekian order (Hebrews 7:13–14).
      - If His sacrifice were not perfect, it would need repeating (Hebrews 10:1–2).
      - If the Old Covenant were sufficient, there would be no need for a new priest or covenant (Hebrews 8:7).
      - If He were not sinless, He could not offer Himself for others (Hebrews 7:26–27).

  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Guarantees the permanence of redemption and forgiveness (Hebrews 9:12).
    - Secures direct and ongoing access to God (Hebrews 4:16; 10:19–22).
    - Ensures continual representation before the Father (Romans 8:34; 1 John 2:1).
    - Brings the Old Covenant to completion and replaces it with the better covenant (Hebrews 8:6–13).
    - Confirms that believers need no other priest or mediator (1 Timothy 2:5).

  - Considerations:
    - Exemptions:
      - Jesus’ priesthood is not inherited, ritualistic, or limited by death (Hebrews 7:23–24).
    
    - Limitations:
      - His priesthood applies to those who come to God through Him (Hebrews 7:25).
      - Priestly intercession does not negate the necessity of repentance and faith (Hebrews 10:26–27).

    - Inferences:
      - Jesus’ priesthood is both sacrificial and intercessory—He offers and advocates.
      - The heavenly sanctuary is the true template; the earthly was a shadow (Hebrews 8:5; 9:24).
      - His continuing priesthood assures the believer of ongoing grace, mercy, and cleansing (Hebrews 4:14–16).

    - Alerts:
      - Avoid reliance on human priesthoods that reintroduce mediatorial roles (Hebrews 10:11–12).
      - Do not treat Christ’s intercession as passive or symbolic; it is real and continual.
      - Reject models that reduce priesthood to mere example or moral function.

    - Corruptions:
      - Roman Catholic view of repeated Eucharistic sacrifice undermines the once-for-all nature of Christ’s offering.
      - Denial of Christ’s priesthood (e.g., some Unitarian or liberal theologies) leaves believers without a mediator.
      - Legalistic systems that reintroduce Old Covenant rituals distort the sufficiency of His priesthood (Galatians 3:1–3).
      - Gnostic rejections of Christ’s bodily offering invalidate the priestly framework (Hebrews 10:5).

6. All Authority and Judgment in heaven, on earth, and under the earth as Messianic Davidic King and Judge of All

  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Show that Jesus is the exalted Son of David, seated at God’s right hand with all authority in heaven and on earth.
    - Present Jesus as the reigning Messiah who fulfills the royal covenant with David and now rules over a universal and eternal kingdom.
    - Emphasize His future return in power and glory to judge the living and the dead.
    - Key texts: 2 Samuel 7:12–14; Psalm 2; Psalm 110; Daniel 7:13–14; Matthew 28:18; Acts 2:29–36; Philippians 2:9–11; Revelation 19–20.

  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:
    - The Son of David (Matthew 1:1; 21:9)
    - The King of kings and Lord of lords (Revelation 19:16)
    - The Son of Man with dominion (Daniel 7:13–14)
    - The Root and Offspring of David (Revelation 22:16)
    - The Judge of the living and the dead (Acts 10:42; 2 Timothy 4:1)
    - The Anointed One set on Zion (Psalm 2:6–12)
    - The one who has all authority (Matthew 28:18)

  - Source, Cause or Warrant for Event, Operation, Methodology or having Attribute
    - God promised David an everlasting throne through his offspring (2 Samuel 7:12–14).
    - Jesus was born of David’s line and declared the heir to that promise (Luke 1:32–33; Romans 1:3).
    - After His resurrection, He was exalted to the right hand of God and enthroned as Lord and Christ (Acts 2:30–36).
    - Jesus claims universal authority in heaven and on earth (Matthew 28:18).
    - The nations are given to Him as inheritance and He rules with a rod of iron (Psalm 2:8–9; Revelation 2:26–27).
    - Judgment is entrusted to the Son because He is the Son of Man (John 5:22, 27).

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:  
    - Affirmation:
      - Jesus is addressed as the Son of David and does not deny it (Matthew 21:9; 22:42).
      - He is declared Lord and Christ by Peter after His resurrection (Acts 2:36).
      - He sits at the Father’s right hand (Hebrews 1:3; Ephesians 1:20–22).
      - All powers and authorities are subject to Him (1 Peter 3:22).
      - He will return in glory and sit on His glorious throne to judge (Matthew 25:31–32).
      - Every knee will bow and every tongue confess Him as Lord (Philippians 2:10–11; Isaiah 45:23).

    - Negation or Rebuttal:
      - If Jesus were not raised and exalted, He could not reign or judge (Acts 17:31; 1 Corinthians 15:25).
      - If He were only a prophet, He could not fulfill the eternal throne promise to David (2 Samuel 7).
      - If He had not ascended, He would not be seated in heavenly authority (Ephesians 4:10).
      - If judgment remained with the Father alone, Jesus could not speak of all judgment being given to the Son (John 5:22).
      - If He were not divine, universal submission to His name would be idolatrous (Isaiah 45:23; Philippians 2:11).

  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Establishes Jesus as the fulfillment of all royal messianic expectation.
    - Confirms that Jesus rules now and not only in the future (1 Corinthians 15:25).
    - Grounds the legitimacy of Christian obedience and mission under His lordship (Matthew 28:18–20).
    - Secures divine justice through a human judge who is righteous and impartial (Acts 17:31).
    - Confirms that history is moving toward a consummation under Christ’s rule (Revelation 11:15).

  - Considerations:
    - Exemptions:
      - Jesus is not a political or territorial king; His kingdom is not of this world (John 18:36).
    
    - Limitations:
      - His rule is currently mediated spiritually through the Church, and visibly awaits consummation at His return (Hebrews 2:8).
      - Judgment has not yet been executed in full but will be revealed at His second coming (Revelation 20:11–15).

    - Inferences:
      - The messianic kingdom is already inaugurated though not yet fully visible (Luke 17:20–21).
      - All human authorities are ultimately subject to Christ, whether they acknowledge Him or not (Psalm 2:10–12; Romans 13:1).
      - Jesus' authority is the basis for the Church’s mission and confidence (Matthew 28:18–20).

    - Alerts:
      - Avoid reducing Christ’s reign to a future-only reality.
      - Do not confuse national Israel's kingdom expectations with Christ’s universal rule.
      - Beware of separating His kingship from His role as judge and ruler of all nations.

    - Corruptions:
      - Postmillennial or political theologies that conflate Christ’s reign with earthly power structures.
      - Dispensational schemes that postpone Christ’s kingship or separate it from His present role.
      - Secular interpretations that recast Jesus as only a moral or revolutionary figure.
      - Denials of final judgment that undermine His judicial authority (universalism, annihilationism).

7. Father and Son with us through the Spirit until the End of the Age

  - Purpose / Target / Goal / Direction
    - Demonstrate that the continued presence of the risen Christ and the Father with the Church is realized through the Holy Spirit.
    - Emphasize the Spirit’s role as the indwelling, empowering, guiding, and comforting presence of God until Christ’s visible return.
    - Show that this presence fulfills Jesus’ promise to be with His people always and anticipates the final union of God with humanity.
    - Key texts: John 14:16–23; Acts 2:1–4; Romans 8:9–11, 26–27; 1 Corinthians 3:16; Revelation 21:3.

  - A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:
    - The Spirit of Truth (John 14:17)
    - The Helper / Paraclete / Comforter (John 14:16; 15:26)
    - The Spirit of Christ (Romans 8:9)
    - The down payment / guarantee of our inheritance (Ephesians 1:13–14)
    - The One who makes us God's dwelling (1 Corinthians 3:16; Ephesians 2:22)
    - The seal of adoption and sonship (Romans 8:15–16)
    - The abiding presence of Christ with His people (Matthew 28:20; John 14:18–23)

  - Source, Cause or Warrant for Event, Operation, Methodology or having Attribute
    - Jesus promised that after His ascension, He would send the Spirit from the Father (John 14:16–17; John 16:7).
    - At Pentecost, the promised Spirit was poured out upon the Church (Acts 2:1–4, 33).
    - The Spirit indwells all who belong to Christ, marking them as His (Romans 8:9; 1 Corinthians 6:19–20).
    - The Spirit mediates the presence of both the Father and the Son (John 14:23; Romans 8:11).
    - The Spirit leads, teaches, convicts, empowers, and intercedes (John 16:13; Romans 8:14, 26–27).
    - The Spirit is the guarantee of our future inheritance and resurrection (Ephesians 1:13–14; Romans 8:11).

  - Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place or Presence of Attribute:  
    - Affirmation:
      - Jesus said He would not leave His disciples as orphans but would come to them (John 14:18).
      - The Spirit came visibly at Pentecost and continues to fill believers (Acts 2; Galatians 4:6).
      - Believers are described as temples of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 3:16; 6:19).
      - The Spirit enables believers to confess Christ, call God Father, and walk in holiness (1 Corinthians 12:3; Romans 8:14–16).
      - The Church grows in unity and power through the Spirit (Ephesians 4:3–4; Acts 9:31).

    - Negation or Rebuttal:
      - If the Spirit were not given, Jesus' promise to be with His people would fail (Matthew 28:20; John 14:16).
      - If the Spirit did not dwell within believers, they would not belong to Christ (Romans 8:9).
      - If the Spirit were not divine, He could not indwell, sanctify, or reveal the mind of God (1 Corinthians 2:10–11).
      - If God were not with His people now, the Church would be powerless and blind (John 15:5; Romans 8:26).
      - If the Spirit were absent, there could be no true union, no guidance, no holiness (Galatians 5:16–25).

  - Accounts For (what outcomes, consequences, or effects are resolved, enabled, or expressed):  
    - Enables the Church to live in ongoing communion with Christ and the Father (John 14:23).
    - Empowers believers for witness, endurance, and sanctification (Acts 1:8; Galatians 5:22–23).
    - Confirms God's permanent presence with His people until final consummation (Revelation 21:3).
    - Assures believers of their identity, security, and future inheritance (Romans 8:16–17; Ephesians 1:14).
    - Makes possible spiritual gifts and fruit for the building up of the body (1 Corinthians 12; Romans 12).

  - Considerations:
    - Exemptions:
      - The Spirit is not selectively given to elite believers but indwells all who are in Christ (Romans 8:9; 1 Corinthians 12:13).

    - Limitations:
      - The Spirit's ministry does not override Scripture or contradict Christ’s teaching (John 16:13–14).
      - The Spirit does not act independently of the Son or the Father (John 16:13–15).

    - Inferences:
      - The age of the Church is the age of the Spirit—marked by indwelling rather than external visitation.
      - The Spirit’s presence is the guarantee that God’s plan is actively unfolding in the present age.
      - The communion of saints is not a social construct but a Spirit-formed body (1 Corinthians 12:13).

    - Alerts:
      - Avoid reducing the Spirit to a force or influence; He is a divine person.
      - Beware of pursuing experience over truth—Spirit and Word never divide.
      - Do not isolate Jesus’ ascension as departure; it enabled this greater nearness (John 16:7).

    - Corruptions:
      - Modalism: collapses distinctions between Father, Son, and Spirit.
      - Pentecostal excesses: prioritize spectacle or emotion over the Word and sanctification.
      - Cessationism: denies or minimizes the present ministry of the Spirit beyond conversion.
      - Liberal theology: redefines the Spirit as human potential or moral conscience rather than divine person.
