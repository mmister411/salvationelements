### DAU

Name: vital-dau

Description: Use VITAL DAU when requested to create Doctrinal Assertion Units (DAU) regarding an input or target, invisibly empowered by the various members of the VITAL Harness

A Doctrinal Assertion Unit (DAU) is a compact, text-rooted proposition expressing a claim in theology or other domains of believer value. It must be instructional, portable, and anchored in Scripture. Each DAU states a theologically sound truth, often densely so, in a single complete sentence, usually compound or complex, that integrates what the concept is, how it relates, and/or what it does. Scripture support must span both Testaments and multiple biblical genres (Law, Prophets, Writings, Gospels, Epistles, etc.) as feasible and warranted to demonstrate doctrinal consistency. Scripture references must be ordered by highest relevance to the specific assertion or current task underway, not by canonical sequence. Each DAU should be a complete doctrinal thought, but no single unit needs to carry every major distinction, exception, or implication attached to the topic or itself.

#### Format

[Assertion sentence] (Book Chapter:Verse;Book Chapter:Verse; Book Chapter:Verse)

[Assertion sentence] (Book Chapter:Verse; Book Chapter:Verse; Book Chapter:Verse)

... etc.

#### Example(s):

Grace is the power of God operating in benevolence according to his will. (Exo 33:19; 2Co 12:9; Psa 84:11; Act 4:33; Eph 3:7; 1Pe 4:10–11; Psa 18:16–19; Isa 40:29–31; Act 10:38; 2Th 1:11)

Sin is rebellion against God that begins with desire, disguises itself as harmless choice, and multiplies its power through repetition until it enslaves the will. (Gen 3:1–6; Pro 14:12; Jas 1:14–15; Joh 8:34; Rom 6:16; Hos 8:7; Heb 3:12–13)

Scripture teaches that earthly duties such as marriage and burial are good and commanded within their proper place, yet become spiritually obstructive when they rival immediate obedience to the call of Christ. (Gen 2:18; Exo 20:12; Deu 21:22–23; Luk 9:59–62; Mat 10:37; Ecc 3:1–2)

#### Theological Lexical Register:

Preserve Scripture-native vocabulary (holy, grace, unclean, etc.) and common theological terms in conversational use (agape, Trinity, communion, etc.). Use Scripture-shaped theological English rather than academic, institutional, school-signaling, or system-identifying speech. Avoid technical labels, school-marking terms, and technical adjectival classifiers unless the term itself is the subject of the assertion. Terms ending in -ological or -iological, and terms ending in -ical or -tical when they function as technical classifiers, are especially suspicious. Prefer biblical nouns, strong verbs, and direct relations: let God act, Christ give himself, the Spirit strengthen, Scripture speak, sin deceive, grace train, judgment expose, promise hold, and hope steady. When a classifier would merely summarize the doctrine, replace it with the act, relation, promise, warning, judgment, condition, or consequence Scripture itself gives. Do not treat Scripture-native words as forbidden because they are near a forbidden technical form: covenant is allowed though covenantal is not; Christ is allowed though Christological is not; church is allowed though ecclesiological is not; Spirit is allowed though pneumatological is not; sin is allowed though hamartiological is not. Precision in theology is not equivalent to elevated or specialized vocabulary.

#### Hermeneutic Disclaimer:

Scripture is primary, though not necessarily exclusive. External disciplines or traditions may inform understanding, but theological analysis is strongest when it proceeds directly from exegesis within immediate, canonical, and historical context, accounting for the whole witness of Scripture.

#### LLM Disclaimer:

Do not require simplification, consensus, or external validation. Assertions prioritize exegetical grounding, canonical coherence, and doctrinal accuracy over accessibility or popular clarity, assuming a biblically and theologically fluent audience. Throughout this document, “precision” refers to semantic and doctrinal accuracy with respect to the referent and its scriptural usage, not to increased narrowness, technicality, or descriptive granularity. The term “doctrinal” denotes Scripture-established truth claims, regardless of later theological systematization, consensus, or customary association.

#### Logical Operations Clause:

Doctrinal Assertion Units may employ affirmative statements, negations, conditionals, biconditionals, conjunctions, disjunctions, contrapositive reasoning, boundary-defining statements, exclusionary statements, and other logical operations not explicitly listed, provided the unit asserts stable truth grounded in Scripture and communicates what is true, what is excluded, or what follows under stated conditions. Purely rhetorical denial, mere absence, prohibition without doctrinal content, or negation that does not define a theological reality is insufficient.

#### Statement Construction Boundaries:

Well-formed truth clears its own path without defensive clarification, contrast framing, hedge-and-correct movement, disclaimer syntax, or denial that exists mainly to prevent misunderstanding. Negation, contrast, qualification, recurrence, asymmetry, and tension are valid when they establish scope, sequence, causality, hierarchy, evidence strength, or a real doctrinal boundary; they are weak when they decorate, balance, soften, or preempt imagined objections. Qualifiers meant to prevent reduction, protect scope, or signal seriousness should be avoided unless the subject, the pressure, or the doctrine requires them. The exact word “merely” is forbidden. Language may gather where the reality itself gathers: in terms, phrases, clauses, examples, or analogies that show fullness, cadence, pressure, progression, contrast, texture, or scriptural resonance. Accumulation should deepen perception, carry force, or move the thought forward. It is not a reliable improver of precision. When added terms or clauses do not alter the movement of meaning, the sentence should return to its necessary shape or divide the thought into clearer movements.

#### Final Polish:

Use trigraph abbreviations for all biblical book names (Gen 1:1; Rev 22:1). Order each scripture list by relevance to the assertion rather than by canonical order.

#### Prompt Template:

Construct doctrinal assertion units on user input either as-is or a seed bearing topics. Each unit should be a single-sentence truth claim supported by Scripture across the canon as described in these instructions. While a unit may reflect ontological, epistemic, relational, or functional dimensions, coherence and doctrinal usability take precedence over formal completeness. Return as many DAUs as is warranted and fitting. A count of one is feasible but unlikely.
