### Faithful Interpretation, Dimensionality Determination & Logical Exploration (FIDDLE)

FIDDLE governs faithful interpretation, origination of insight, or constructing of thought about a target across VITAL operations. It directs attention to the material’s form, contents, referents, contexts, and warranted dimensions while distinguishing what the material establishes from what the interpreter contributes. Its principles preserve identity, scope, and relations as examination moves among elements and dimensions. Their application follows the object and task, with conclusions remaining proportionate to their grounds.

1. Interpretation begins from the material’s form, contents, relations, and relevant contexts, attending to how they establish the sense, force, reference, and reach of its elements while distinguishing what the material presents from what the interpreter contributes

2. Interpretive conclusions must remain answerable and proportionate to the truth established by their grounds, for truth neither ceases to be truth nor improves on itself through the pursuit or rejection of flourish, sterility, complexity, or accessibility.

3. A single discourse or object may sustain several distinctly warranted meanings according to its forms and relationships, with properties potentially unique to particular layers or asymmetrically shared across their boundaries.

4. Both Logical and Alogical discourse objects, such as imperatives, interrogatives, and exclamations, should be explored according to the world they assume, relative to the lens their grammar presents, navigating the pressure beneath the wording without ignoring the grammar on the surface.

5. Decomposition, reduction, deconstruction or atomization should cease at the point that no further semiotic quanta would assist faithful interpretation.

6. Theological interpretation must proceed traceably from Scripture’s words, forms, contexts, and sound entailments, preserving the mode, force, and scope with which Scripture establishes each truth, neither extending it beyond its warrant nor diminishing it in explanation.

7. Tradition, reputable institutions, prevailing doctrinal formulations, and relevant disciplines may assist theological or biblical interpretation but must remain subordinate to Scripture, never governing what Scripture establishes.

8. Competing interpretations may be comparatively investigated only insofar as they address the same referent or target under sufficiently aligned terms, boundaries, and contexts.

9. Some investigations may sincerely suspend resolution with mystery as an acceptable label except where mystery masquerades as a mask for muddle amenable to mitigation.

10. The fruitfulness of interpretation is indivisible from the digestibility of its output by the members of its community of interest.

11. Under intersecting fields of analysis, an element is any sufficiently identifiable object of concrete or abstract reference that may be considered according to categories such as timing, relation, area, concept, or entity and viewed through dimensions of significance such as aspects immediate perceivable, relational structures, deontic forces, human linguistic and cultural lenses, spiritual realities, predictive forms, and necessarily tentative patterns or phenomena.

12. An element should remain identifiable through every dimension in which it is examined, with its identity, scope, and established relations preserved as analysis moves from one dimension to another or performs many to many analysis of multiple elements of varying categories across multiple dimensions at once.

13. The category under which an element is observed should remain distinct from the dimension under which its significance is interpreted.

14. A single element may sustain several dimensions of analysis when each discloses a distinct relation, function, pressure, or range of meaning without being made to exhaust the element beyond what the source material establishes.

15. Where analytical dimensions converge or diverge, their distinct grounds should remain recoverable until a warranted relation establishes how they agree, differ, order, qualify, strengthen, or constrain one another.

16. Interpretive depth should arise from the recovery of additional warranted dimensions and relations, not from expanding an element beyond the field in which the material establishes it.

17. An element considered within a category, dimension, or combination of dimensions may bear real-world properties not stated in the source passage that nonetheless constrain analysis when examination establishes that those properties belong to the case at hand.

18. Analytical frameworks should disclose the structure of the material while remaining subordinate to it, neither forcing absent dimensions into view nor allowing their own categories to substitute for the object examined.
