#### Humans

The human soul persists consciously beyond bodily death, retaining personal identity, awareness, memory, and the capacity for experience. Its non-mortality bears similarities to the continuing existence of other spiritual beings while retaining the distinctions proper to human personhood and nature.

Tripartite Anthropology - The human person consists of body, soul, and spirit, each distinguishable in nature and operation while together constituting one human being. Human existence, identity, and spiritual condition involve all three, whose distinct capacities and relationships bear upon life, death, corruption, and redemption. The human physical body, or soma, develops according to its divinely designed physical processes, while the breath proceeding from God becomes the human pneuma. Their chemo-spiritual interaction gives rise to the psuche, the persistent human person. The soma and pneuma are distinguishable vehicles through which the person participates in the physical and spiritual realms respectively. The psuche retains the person's identity through changes in the conditions and availability of those vehicles.

Human testimony is a valid evidentiary pathway to knowledge of reality. Its content deserves substantive assessment according to its claims, coherence, circumstances, and available evidence, without presuming fault, deception, or incapacity in the witness. Testimony carries informational value in its own right, and its truth is determined by correspondence with reality.

No accountable human is self-representatively innocent, vanishingly few are volitionally villainous, and every single soul from miscarriage to minister to maniac has intrinsic value through bearing the image of God.

God judges every person impartially according to actual knowledge, capacities, opportunities, conduct, and spiritual condition. Those who receive the Gospel may endure faithfully or succumb to apostasy and corruption. Those without the Gospel remain subject to God’s judgment and within reach of Christ’s saving work. Those likely incapable of accountable personal sin require no judicial pardon for offenses never committed, though inherited corruption upon their soul may still require divine restoration. Final judgment yields favorable or unfavorable outcomes. Those judged favorably may receive differing inheritances and privileges according to God’s righteous disposition.

Christ’s finished work prevents inherited Adamic corruption from making human failure automatically decisive. Deliberately embracing corruption remains consequential. Every corruption must finally be cleansed, corrected, or consigned to perdition. Creatures that love corruption, which unfortunately includes those who are on the Vine as branches in Christ and includes many out in the world timeline, cannot be simply cleansed, have become themselves corrupted, and will be cast into perdition. Whoever resists their corruption and presses to light has a real opportunity for escape through the finished work of Christ.
	
Righteous conduct reflects an inward disposition that aligns with God to the extent that His nature and the integrity He requires having been revealed. For those who know the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, the requirement is the fullness of the fear of the Lord, agape, faith, obedience, repentance, forgiveness, and rejection of evil. Such a disposition loves what God loves, hates what God hates, receives correction, and acts according to received truth. Commendable works cannot neutralize a settled love of corruption, because what a creature loves changes the creature itself. For those who have not known the God of Israel, scripture is not silent while also not definitive. God works with them according to His divine will, drawing on the extent to which they recognize agape love toward the image of God and recognize the fingerprints of the Eternal God on creation. More explicitly, it is a comparison between what was written in the law and how they handle their local undertakings. The crux of the law is agape for God and agape for others. "He will have mercy on whom He will have mercy." "Whoever loves their brother knows him."

Salvation is God’s unfolding work from before creation into eternity, permitting freedom to bring forth creatures in His image who become forever His loving allies, friends, and family. Its security rests in God’s faithful power received through living faith. The Father keeps believers, Christ intercedes for them, and the Holy Spirit strengthens them as they abide in Christ, resist sin, repent, and endure.
