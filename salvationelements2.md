# The Seven Elements of Salvation

## Introduction

This document aims to provide an in-depth understanding of the elements of salvation according to scripture, breaking them down into functional terms that are easy to grasp. Salvation is not a one-time event but a journey, nor a monolithic force but a process that involves various aspects of spiritual transformation and growth. This guide is structured around seven key layers, each representing a significant aspect of the salvation dynamic. Each section will delve into the theological terms associated with these layers, the scriptural basis for each, and the practical implications for a believer's life. Whether you are new to the Christian faith or seeking a deeper understanding of your spiritual journey, this guide aims to be a valuable resource for you.

### Hermeneutical Disclaimer

This analysis proceeds on the methodological assumption that theology must arise traceably from exegesis of the biblical text. (Acts 17:11; 2 Tim 2:15; Neh 8:8). As we see when Jesus proved the resurrection from "I AM the God of Abraham" in the book of Exodus, many truths of scripture are informally entailed rather than formally demanded. Faithful exegesis attends to tense, number, and wording because the Spirit’s choice of words bears doctrinal weight, and therefore even the grammar of Scripture can establish truths as great as resurrection. (Mat 22:31–32; Gal 3:16; Joh 10:35; 2Pe 1:20–21; Pro 30:5–6; Deu 32:46–47) We must balance that with setting that all readings and conclusions must respect immediate literary and historical context while also being apt to consider broader material from the given book and canon where applicable (Luke 24:27; Acts 28:23). All scriptural content and reality must be held as true at every point, all the time in at least one or more semiotic, semantic or logical avenue. “True, but…” is to be excessively scrutinized. (2Ti 3:16; Joh 17:17; Psa 119:160; Num 23:19; Tit 1:2) Systematic theology, interpretive tradition, naming conventions, science, archaeology,  philosophy, and other public endeavors possess intrinsic value as disciplines (Col 2:8; 1Co 2:12–13; Pro 25:2; Dan 1:17). Engagement with these disciplines must not introduce meanings unreasonably foreign to the Word’s original context (Matt 15:9; Col 2:8), exceed what the text soundly and cogently supports (1 Cor 4:6), or contradict what the given text explicitly states, or other texts in the totality of scripture (Deut 4:2; Prov 30:6; Rev 22:18–19). Confessions, councils, catechisms, creeds, and church statements serve as subordinate witnesses that summarize and guard the faith once delivered, yet they possess authority only insofar as they agree with the God-breathed Scripture, which alone is the final rule, judge, and standard of all teaching. (2Ti 3:16–17; Act 17:11; Isa 8:20; Gal 1:8–9; 1Co 4:6; Mat 15:3,6; 2Pe 1:19–21) Interpretive lenses are necessarily embedded in all handling of Scripture but must not outweigh the text’s own logos, ethos, or pathos (2 Cor 4:2; Ps 119:130; Heb 4:12).

### Trichotomy View Disclaimer

While both the two-part view of man, commonly called dichotomy, and the three-part view of man, commonly called trichotomy, are biblically plausible, our study has led us to use trichotomy as the clearest framework for describing salvation according to the Scriptures. We understand man as a living soul who has a physical body for earthly life and a human spirit formed by God for spiritual relation and life before Him. Within this framework, inherited corruption from Adam’s fall, personal sin prior to the cleansing blood of Jesus, and the sin of those who have come to know Jesus may be understood as affecting the soul, the earthly body, and the human spirit in distinct ways, including the distinction between the initial human spirit and the spirit made alive in new birth. This framework is used here as an interpretive aid, without intended implication or boundary of fellowship or a denial that faithful believers may hold the dichotomous view.
(1 Thes. 5:23; Heb. 4:12; Gen. 2:7; 1 Cor. 15:44–49; 2 Cor. 5:1–4; Zech. 12:1; Rom. 5:12–19; Eph. 2:1–3; Rom. 7:22–25)

#### Salvation defined in short

Salvation is often simplified into a single, monolithic operation that either simply is or is not, always was or never was. The preceding analysis treats salvation as dynamic because the dynamic God has produced a dynamic creation, tragically affected in dynamic ways by sin. Differences in how one describes salvation’s function are not, by themselves, the measure of one’s salvation; nevertheless, clarity about function is necessary for honest discourse and shared study.

Salvation is the unfolding arc of divine operation, spanning from before creation into the eternal future, by which God permits freedom to run its course in order to bring forth creatures in His image who, though once His enemies, become forever His loving allies, friends, and family. It has an already, ongoing, and not-yet form: believers have been saved by grace, are being saved as they hold fast the word, and will be saved from wrath and death when Christ appears. Its security is the faithful power of God received through living faith. Christ intercedes, the Spirit strengthens, and the Father keeps believers as they abide in Christ, continue in the faith, resist sin, repent when they fall, and endure to the end.

This security is real, but it is not mechanical possession apart from living covenantal union. Those truly sanctified by the blood may yet profane that blood by willful rebellion, abandon the Lord who bought them, fall from grace, be severed from Christ, and return to destruction unless they repent. Salvation turns enemies into allies, strangers into household members, slaves into sons, rebels into witnesses, and the alienated into friends of Christ, until those who overcome become forever the loving family of God and servants of the Lamb in the new creation.

(Eph 1:3–10; Rom 8:28–30; Col 1:19–22; John 15:1–15; 1 John 3:1–2; Rev 21:3–7; Gen 1:26–28; 2 Tim 1:9–10; Rom 5:6–11; Isa 43:6–7; Heb 2:10–13; Rev 22:3–5; Heb 10:26–31; 2 Pet 2:1, 20–22; Gal 5:4; Col 1:21–23; 1 Cor 15:1–2; Rom 11:20–22; Heb 3:6–14; Heb 6:4–8; Rev 2:10; Eph 1:7–14; Rom 8:18–25; Titus 2:11–14; Ezek 18:21–32; Luke 15:11–32.)

## The Elements of Salvation in Functional Terms

- Renewed Human-Spirit Breathed anew from God
  - **A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:**
    - Born-Again (John 3:3, 1 Peter 1:23)
    - Regeneration (Ezekiel 11:19, Titus 3:5)
    - New-Birth (Psalm 102:18, Ezekiel 36:25-26, Jeremiah 31:31-34, Isaiah 44:3, Isaiah 55:3, 2 Corinthians 5:17)
    - Quickening (Titus 3:5, Ephesians 2:4-5, Psalm 119:50)
  - **Source or Cause of Event**
    - Holy Sprit breaths new breath into Person (Genesis 2:7; Job 33:4; John 20:22; Acts 2:2-4; Romans 8:11)
  - **Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place:**
    - Affirmation:
      - Accepting Truth-of-GOD received (2 Thessalonians 2:10-12, John 8:32)
      - Living by Agape (1 John 4:7-8,16, John 15:12)
    - Negation
      - Not being an Atheist (Psalm 14:1, Romans 1:20, Hebrews 11:6, Proverbs 1:7)
      - Not being directly opposed to the God of Abraham, Isaac, & Jacob (Exodus 3:6, 15; Matthew 22:32; Acts 3:13; Romans 4:16)
      - Not being directly opposed to the Jesus of Nazareth (Matthew 10:33; Luke 12:8-9; 1 John 2:23; 2 Timothy 2:12)
  - Accounts For:
    - Carrying the Soul of the to the Gates of the City with Foundations upon physical death (Ecclesiastes 12:7, Psalm 146:4, 1 John 5:4, Ecclesiastes 3:21, Philippians 1:23)
    - Reopening clear communication and ability to commune with the LORD (Hebrews 4:16, 1 Timothy 2:5)
  - Considerations:
    - Exemptions: 
      - Infants, Children, cognitively impaired, or others unable to commit personal sin, as their original spirits are not altered by personal sin (Matthew 18:3, 2 Samuel 12:23, Deuteronomy 1:39).
    - Limitations:
      - New Birth (or having a human spirit unencumbered by personal sin) is an absolute requirement but does not ensure entry into the Kingdom of God. (Matthew 13:3-23, Matthew 25:1-13, Hebrews 3:14)
      - New Birth is necessary function of new covenant but exists before it as a requirement for kingdom entry across redemptive history. (Deuteronomy 10:16, 30:6; Psalm 51:10, Ezek 11:19–20)
    - Inferences:
      - All labled or refered to as righteous, upright, qodeshim, or the congregation from the old testament were born-again, lest these not see the kingdom of God (Hebrews 11, Psalm 1:5; Habakkuk 2:4)
    - Alert:
      - There will be many who experienced the new birth but are cast into perdition for loving darkness despite the measure of light they've known. (John 3:19, Romans 2:13-15, Matthew 7:21–23, John 15:2, 6; 2 Peter 2:20–22)
- Judicial Pardon
  - A.K.A./Assoc. With:
    - Justification, Imputed Righteousness (Romans 5:1, Titus 3:7, Romans 4:22-24, 2 Corinthians 5:21)
    - Redemption, Propitiation (Romans 3:25, 1 John 2:2, Ephesians 1:7, 1 Peter 1:18-19)
    - Sins Forgiven, Absolution (Ephesians 1:7, Colossians 2:13-14, John 20:23, James 5:15
    - Reconciliation (2 Corinthians 5:18-19, Romans 5:10)
  - **Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place:**
    - Having perosnally sinned (Romans 3:23; Romans 5:12; 1 John 1:8-10; Ecclesiastes 7:20)
    - Valid Blood Atonement (Hebrews 9:22, Romans 3:25)
    - Forgiving Others (Matthew 6:14-15, Ephesians 4:32)
    - Repentance (Acts 3:19, Luke 13:3)
    - Faith
  - Accounts for:
    - Criminal Status with the Court of Heaven (Romans 8:1, Psalm 32:1-2)
    - Removing Offense-related blockage to relationship with GOD (Isaiah 59:2, Psalm 103:12)
    - Erasing the record of sin (Isaiah 43:25, Acts 3:19)
  - Considerations
    - Exemptions:
      - Infants, cognitively impaired, or others unable to commit personal sin are exempt due to their personal innocence in the context of justice (Matthew 19:14, Luke 18:16, James 4:17, Romans 4:15).
    - Limitations: 
      - While the blood of Jesus covers all sin, Judicial Pardon does not address other spiritual conditions rendering one unable to enter the Strait Gate (Matthew 13:3-23, Matthew 18:21-35, Matthew 25:14-30, Heb 12:14).
    - Inferences:
      - [1] The Lamb was slain from the foundation of the world; [2] He is the only God formed; [3] He has mercy on whom He wills; Therefore the force behind all sacrifices, faith and repentance has always been the blood of the lamb. (Revelation 13:8; Hebrews 9:12-15; Romans 3:25; 1 Peter 1:19-20)
    - Alert:
      - Unforgiveness in all forms threatens to render the soul incompatible with Judicial Pardon of personal sins. (Matthew 6:14–15; Matthew 18:21–35; Hebrews 12:15; 1 Peter 4:8)
- Decontaminating the Soul
  - **A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:**
    - Renewing of the Mind (Romans 12:2, Ephesians 4:23)
    - Heart-of-Flesh replacing Heart-of-Stone (Ezekiel 36:26, Hebrews 8:10)
    - Circumcision of the Heart (Deuteronomy 10:16; 30:6; Romans 2:29; Colossians 2:11)
    - Cleansing/Healing the Conscience (Hebrews 10:22, 1 Timothy 1:5)
  - Source or Cause of Event
    - Blood Of Jesus (1 John 1:7; Revelation 1:5; Hebrews 9:14; 1 Peter 1:18-19; Ephesians 1:7)
    - Word of GOD (John 17:17; Ephesians 5:26; Psalm 119:9; John 15:3; 1 Peter 1:23)
    - Indwelling Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19; Romans 8:9-11; Galatians 5:16; 2 Corinthians 3:18)
  - **Requirement for or Confirmation of Event Taking Place:**
    - Affirmation
      - Blood of Jesus (1 John 1:7; Revelation 1:5; Hebrews 9:14; Hebrews 10:22; 1 Peter 1:18–19; Isaiah 1:18)
      - Advocacy (Hebrews 7:25; Philippians 4:6; James 5:16; Galatians 6:2; Isaiah 53:4; Matthew 25:40; Proverbs 31:8–9; 1 Thessalonians 5:14; Romans 12:15)
      - Watering (Ephesians 5:26, John 7:37–39, Philippians 4:6, Hebrews 10:24–25, 1 Corinthians 10:16, Psalm 1:2–3).
    - Negation
      - Remaining Unspotted from the World (James 1:27; 1 John 2:15–17; Romans 12:2; 2 Peter 1:4; 2 Corinthians 6:17; 2 Timothy 2:21; Titus 2:11–12)
  - Accounts For:
    - Cleanliness required to withstand the presence of the LORD (Psalm 24:3-4, 2 Corinthians 6:17)
    - Holiness required to See the LORD (Hebrews 12:14, 1 Peter 1:15-16)
    - Ability to Enter in at the Strait Gate of the City with Foundations (Matthew 7:13-14, Luke 13:24)
  - Considerations:
    - Exemptions: 
      - None due to the inherent uncleaness of all souls by way of the first Adam (Romans 5:12; Psalm 51:5)
    - Limitation: 
      - Initial and repeat decontamination are given freely by grace but loving darkness is resisting the gift, allowing for recontamination (John 3:19–20)
      - Decontamination is impermanent like the oil of a burning lamp and needs to be tended (Matthew 25:1–13)
    - Alert: 
      - Soulical Cleanliness is sensitive to holy Fear of the Lord (Proverbs 8:13, Proverbs 16:6) and not grieving or quenching the Holy Spirit (Ephesians 4:30, 1 Thessalonians 5:19)
      - Soulical contamination is relative to each person with some dangers being universal such as but not limited to unforgiveness, drinking blood or mixing Jesus with other spirituality, especially occultism or mystery religion (Acts 15:20, 29; 21:25; 1 Corinthians 10:20-21; 2 Corinthians 6:14-17; Deuteronomy 18:9-12).
- Fear of the Lord
  - **A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:**
    - Walking in the Spirit & Not the Flesh (Galatians 5:16-17, Romans 8:4)
    - Putting on Christ (Romans 13:14, Galatians 3:27)
    - Putting on the Armor of GOD (Ephesians 6:10-18, 1 Thessalonians 5:8)
    - Love or Hate what God Loves or hates (Psalm 97:10, Proverbs 8:13, Proverbs 16:6)
    - Reverence (Hebrews 12:28; Malachi 1:6)
    - Awe before His majesty or holiness (Isaiah 6:5; Psalm 33:8)
    - Beginning of knowledge and wisdom (Proverbs 1:7; 9:10)
    - Dread of violating or misrepresenting God's name (Leviticus 19:12; Malachi 2:5)
    - Sanctifying the Lord in Your Heart ()
  - Source or Cause of Event, Operation, or Methodology:
    - Initiated by exposure to God's self-revelation in word or act (Deuteronomy 4:10; Exodus 20:18–20)
    - Given by the Spirit of the Lord (Isaiah 11:2–3)
    - Cultivated through learning, remembrance, and reflection on God's works and judgments (Psalm 34:11; Deuteronomy 8:6)
    - Deepened by internal recognition of God's holiness, power, and justice (Psalm 119:120; Isaiah 66:2)
    - Strengthened through community alignment and covenantal teaching (Deuteronomy 6:1–2; Acts 9:31)
  - **Evidence of Validity or Confirmation**
    - **Affirmation:**
      These traits and responses confirm that the Fear of the Lord is present and active:
      - Prioritizing GOD over cares of the world (Matthew 6:33, Luke 10:38-42)
      - Obedience to what you know to be true and good (1 Peter 1:22, James 4:7)
      - Approving what God Loves (Psalm 11:7; Proverbs 15:9; Micah 6:8; 1 John 4:19)
      - Disapproval of evil and withdrawal from it (Proverbs 8:13; 16:6)
      - Submission & discipline to God’s instructions (Job 28:28; Psalm 111:10)
      - Receptive conscience and readiness to repent (Isaiah 66:2; Proverbs 28:14)
      - A lifestyle marked by integrity and upright walking (Proverbs 14:2; Nehemiah 5:15)
      - Reverence in worship and caution in speaking or acting in His name (Ecclesiastes 5:1–2;
    - Negation
      - Not loving the things of the World (1 John 2:15-17, James 4:4)
      - Being not conformed to this present evil age (Romans 12:2, Galatians 1:4)
      - Disproving what God Hates (Proverbs 6:16-19; Psalm 5:4-6; Romans 1:18; Revelation 2:6)
  - Accounts For:
    - Fulfillment of Promises attached to seeking the LORD in obedience (Hebrews 11:6, Psalm 9:10, Deuteronomy 28:1-2, John 15:10)
    - Breathing life into claims of loving the LORD (John 14:23, 1 John 5:3)
    - Not shut out when the door is closed or cast out before the throne (Matthew 25:10-12, Matthew 25:31-46, Luke 13:24-27, Revelation 20:12-15)
    - Being Good Soil (Matthew 13:8, 23; Luke 8:15; Mark 4:20)
  - Considerations:
  - Exceptions:
    - None
  - Limitations
    - Fear of he Lord is a start, an ingredient, a mandatory element but not the whole machine or recipe.(Philippians 2:12–13; Ecclesiastes 12:13; Matthew 23:23)
    - Without ongoing attention, it may fade into familiarity or presumption (Deuteronomy 6:10–12; Psalm 78:11; Revelation 2:4–5)
    - It may exist alongside distance or trembling (James 2:19). It can be momentary or partial if not accompanied by right understanding and trust. (James 2:19; Exodus 20:20; Isaiah 29:13)
    - Parables: Failures of would-be believers outlined in Jesus' parables identify various elements of failing at Holy Fear of the Lord (Matthew 13:3-23, Matthew 25:1-13, Luke 12:16-21)
  - Inference
    - Love v. Fear v. Faith: Ultimately, Loving God, Faith in God, and Fear of The Lord are at least heavily overlapping, if not identical (1 John 4:18, Hebrews 11:6, Proverbs 8:13).
    - Sanctification: Growing in the richness of fear of the Lord is the primary undertaking of sanctification and vice versa (1 Thessalonians 4:3-4, 2 Corinthians 7:1, Proverbs 1:7).
    - Servile fear that paralyzes rather than humbles (Romans 8:15)
    - Fear of man or circumstances disguised as reverence for God (Proverbs 29:25; Isaiah 29:13)
    - External religiosity with no inward awe or repentance (Isaiah 1:11–15; Matthew 15:8–9)
    - Terror without love, leading to avoidance rather than nearness (1 John 4:18)
    - Cultural moralism that mimics uprightness without reference to God’s holiness (2 Timothy 3:5)
- Indwelling of the Holy Spirit
  - **A.K.A. / Associated With / What is Meant By:**
    - The Promise (Acts 1:4-5, Luke 24:49)
    - "The Kingdom is among you" (Luke 17:21, Matthew 12:28)
    - "Whoever does not have Christ's Spirit is none of His." (Romans 8:9, 1 Corinthians 3:16)
  - **Source or Cause of Event, Operation, or Methodology:**
    - Direct provision by the Father at the request of the Son (John 14:16, 26; John 15:26)
    - Entry into the New Covenant through the blood of Christ (Luke 22:20; Hebrews 9:15)
    - Faith in the Eternal Person of Jesus the Christ (Acts 2:38)
    - The Spirit is given to those who hear, believe, and obey (Acts 5:32; Galatians 3:2)
  - **Evidence of Validity or Confirmation**
  - **Affirmation: These indicators serve as signs that the indwelling of the Holy Spirit is present and functionally operative:**
    - Confession of Jesus Christ as the eternal and incarnate Son of God (1 John 4:2–3, John 8:24)
    - Receptive belief in the gospel followed by sealed faith response (Ephesians 1:13, Acts 5:32)
    - Internal witness bearing confirmation to the spirit of the person (Romans 8:16)
    - Emergence of spiritual fruit over time, indicating internal transformation (Galatians 5:22–23)
    - Increasing responsiveness to truth, conviction, correction, and renewal (John 16:13, 2 Corinthians 3:18)
  - **Negation: Evidences of reprobacy regarding the Holy Spirit, whether by non-start or terminal grievance. Concluding one's race of faith in this state is exceptionally, gravely dangerous.**
    - Reprobate unto every good work or active resistance of the Holy Spirit.(Titus 1:16, 2 Timothy 3:5; Acts 7:51; John 14:17; Hebrews 10:26–29)
    - Denial that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, Lord or come in the flesh (1 John 2:22–23; 1 John 4:2–3; 1 Corinthians 12:3)
    - Love of the world overriding love of truth (1 John 2:15–17)
    - Idolatry, Witchcraft, Occult (1 Corinthians 10:20–21, 1 John 4:1)
  - **Accounts for:**
    - The Power to overcome this World (1 John 5:4, Romans 8:37)
    - The Power to be transformed into the likeness of Christ (2 Corinthians 3:18, Romans 12:2)
    - Perpetual access to omnipresent fellowship with the LORD (John 14:16-17, 1 Corinthians 6:19)
    - The internal guidance system for discerning truth and resisting deception (John 16:13, 1 John 2:27)
    - The pledge and down payment guaranteeing future inheritance (Ephesians 1:13–14)
  - Considerations
    - Exemptions:
      - None. The indwelling is not a requirement but a promise. Scripture does, however, seem to speak separately between those who believe on the Son and those who do not encounter the protoevangelium or the revealed gospel, or are too young or not sufficiently mentally present. The Spirit is a requirement for co-heirship with Christ.
    - Limitations:
      - The indwelling does not prevent soulical recontamination from embracing the world or resisting fear of the Lord. (Galatians 5:17; James 4:4; Ephesians 4:30)
      - The Holy Spirit can be (Acts 7:51), quenched (1 Thessalonians 5:19), grieved (Ephesians 4:30), or neglected (1 Timothy 4:14) which may cause dormancy or be functionally annulled in cases of hardened will or habitual rebellion. Including ignoring guidance or prompts, engaging actions or attitudes incompatible with Spirit, and loving the things of this world (Acts 7:51, 1 Timothy 4:14, Matthew 12:31-32, 1 Thessalonians 5:19, Ephesians 4:30)
    - Inference
      - Indwelling functions as a boundary marker for covenant identity (Romans 8:9) and facilitates communion with God, conviction of sin, empowerment for calling, and spiritual discernment (John 16:8–13). The presence of the Spirit is inseparable from the life of the new covenant and anticipates glorification. Lack of indwelling is correlated with disqualification (Romans 8:14; John 14:17).
    - Alert:
      - Persistent rejection of the Spirit's leading, attribution of His work to evil, or treating His presence as casual may result in spiritual ruin (Matthew 12:31–32; Hebrews 10:29). Spiritual decline often begins by silencing conviction. Rebellion or compromise in practice may lead to a forfeiture of power, awareness, and eventually inheritance (2 Peter 2:20–21; Revelation 3:16).
      - The pull toward an evil heart of unbelief in departing form the Living God can be guarded against by an active life of devotion and pressing into the Lord in praise, prayer, worship, time and meditation in His presence.
    - Corruptions: Heterodoxy, Heresy, Misinterpretation, elicit Spirituality, etc.
      - Depersonalization of the Spirit into an impersonal force, energy, or influence (John 14:26; Ephesians 4:30, Romans 8:9; Galatians 3:2)
      - Spiritualized selfism, equating the Spirit with latent human potential or divine spark (2 Corinthians 11:4)
      - Syncretism, integrating the Holy Spirit with ancestral spirits, universal energies, or pantheistic frameworks (2 Corinthians 6:14–16; 1 Corinthians 10:20–21)

### Appendix 2: Novel Parables And Hypotheticals for addressing defensibly inferrible mechanics for the eternal state of the starkly unreached

#### The Case of Shen Ping (沈平): A Hypothetical Historical Unevangelized Person

Consider Shen Ping, a hypothetical Chinese man born in 70 BC who lives in the region of modern Beijing until his death in AD 2. He never encounters the Scriptures of Israel, hears of Abraham or Moses, or receives instruction concerning the covenants God established with their descendants. He dies before the earthly ministry, crucifixion, and resurrection of Jesus Christ, without ever knowing the name by which the Son of God would be revealed to the nations.

Nevertheless, Shen Ping lives in the world God created, beneath the heavens that declare His glory, possessing a conscience capable of recognizing good and evil. The eternal God has not abandoned him to his circumstances. God has placed eternity in the human heart, made His power and divine nature perceptible through creation, and ordered the circumstances of the nations so that people might seek Him and find Him. Shen Ping is among those whom the true light enlightens, and the God of Israel is also the God of the Gentiles. (Rom 1:19–20; Act 17:26–28; Ecc 3:11; Joh 1:9; Psa 19:1–4; Rom 3:29)

Suppose Shen Ping looks upon creation and recognizes an eternal Creator who stands above the world and all that inhabits it. He comes to believe that this God is worthy of reverence, that righteousness and mercy are good, and that cruelty, deception, and selfishness violate what ought to be. He cannot name the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, but he seeks the eternal God according to the truth available to him. He recognizes his own wrongdoing, asks for mercy, and endeavors to live according to the light he possesses. His faith takes shape in conduct. He cares for those in need, refuses opportunities to prosper through cruelty, forgives those who injure him, and responds to the accusations of conscience with repentance rather than self-justification.

Shen Ping remains a sinner. His upright conduct cannot erase his offenses, and his earnestness cannot independently cleanse the corruption inherited through Adam. He requires the saving operations that only God can accomplish.

Shen Ping is in need of the Holy Spirit making the human spirit anew, a sufficient ground of pardon that is only in the sacrifice of Christ and cleansing of the sin's corruption of the soul which is only done by the blood of the Lamb slain form the foundation of the world. Though Christ's sacrifice occurs later in human history, its redemptive efficacy belongs to God's eternal purpose. The Lamb foreknown before the foundation of the world is the same Redeemer through whom God passed over former sins and secured redemption for those who lived before the Cross. (1Pe 1:18–20; Rom 3:23–26; Heb 9:15; Tit 3:5; Eze 36:26–27)

The relevant question is whether Shen Ping's response to the truth God has given him constitutes what the Holy Spirit considers as satisfying being born again. Scripture associates knowing God with agape, connects being born of God with genuine love, and declares that those who approach Him must believe that He exists and rewards those who diligently seek Him. God remains the agent of salvation. Shen Ping's responsiveness is consequential because it concerns his actual relationship to divine truth, while the Spirit's regeneration, Christ's atonement, and the Father's mercy accomplish what no human response can supply by its own power. (1Jo 4:7–8; Heb 11:6; Joh 3:5–8; Gen 4:7; Rom 4:3–8)

Romans 2 places Shen Ping within the jurisdiction of God's impartial judgment. God renders to each person according to their works, distinguishes perseverance in good from obedience to unrighteousness, and acknowledges that Gentiles without the written Law may possess genuine moral knowledge through conscience and the procession of society composted of image-bearers of God. Their thoughts can accuse or excuse them, and God judges the secrets of human beings through Jesus Christ. The chapter's stated outcomes include eternal life, glory, honor, peace, wrath, and tribulation. Shen Ping's lack of access to Israel's written revelation does not remove him from these principles, while his lack of knowledge cannot itself establish the guilt of knowingly rejecting a revelation he never received. (Rom 2:6–16; Rom 2:26–29; Luk 12:47–48; Act 10:34–35)

If Shen Ping's actual belief, alignment to principles of agape, amenableness to repentance, and spiritual condition accord with the favorable case described, God's grace according the purpose, counsil and will of God can preserve him from eternal destruction through the same Christ whose saving work is proclaimed in the gospel. His preservation does not establish that he possesses every particular privilege of the New Covenant, including the indwelling promise and co-inheritance associated with union with Christ in the revealed gospel. Even believers within the nation of Israel or situated in-between the patriarchs and  Sinai cannot be readily assumed to have the same inheritance afforded those who have responded to the later Gospel. Eternal preservation and the particular inheritance of Christ's adopted co-heirs are distinguishable realities. The final judgment determines what Shen Ping receives according to God's righteousness and the saving work accomplished on his behalf. (Rom 8:15–17; Eph 1:13–14; Rom 2:7–11; Rev 21:1–7)

Consider also the consequence if Shen Ping recognizes righteousness yet repeatedly suppresses it, exploits others, embraces cruelty, and hardens himself against conscience and repentance. His ignorance of Israel's Scriptures would not absolve him of rebellion against truth he genuinely recognized. The same impartial judgment remains operative, and persistent love of corruption can bring him to eternal loss. The awareness of this fact emerges in much folk religion around the world and through the ages. God's knowledge of his circumstances includes the light given, the capacities possessed, the opportunities encountered, the conduct undertaken, and the condition in which he finally stands before the Lord. (Rom 1:18–32; Rom 2:8–16; Joh 3:19–21; Eze 18:21–32)

Shen Ping thus illustrates the reach of redemption beyond the historical distribution of its revelation. The Father does not cease to be God of the Gentiles beyond the borders of Israel. The Spirit does not lose His power to make a person anew where the New Covenant has not yet been proclaimed. The blood of Christ does not acquire its first power to redeem when a human being first hears about it. The gospel reveals the decisive work through which salvation has been secured, while God Himself remains present and active among His creatures according to His eternal counsel.

Shen Ping stands before the same God as Abraham, Moses, Peter, and Paul. His knowledge differs, his circumstances differ, and the covenantal privileges available during his earthly life differ. The holiness, agape, righteousness, and faithfulness of the God who judges him do not differ. The God who has given him light knows perfectly what he has done with it, what grace has accomplished within him, and what remains when he stands before the judgment seat of Christ.

#### The Case of Daphne Cooper: A Hypothetical Person Taken Too Young

Ms. Cooper was born 1936 in London to secular parents. Daphne was a pristine baby girl with no birth complications for her or her mother. Her eyes were a mesmerizing hazel. Her hair was a rich brown. Her smile and laugh could light up the solar system. Her first birthday came the following year. She couldn't articulate her favorate color yet or maybe handn't locked it in but she's smile just a bit more at cyan or light blue. So for her birthday she got some trinkets in various shade of blue. A beautiful time for all. The next morning after her birthday her parents came to check on her. At first it seems she had slept in. Upon looking closer, to their horror, Daphne had succumb to sudden infant death syndrome. 366 days old and her time in this world was up.

Daphne had never learned to speak in full sentences, read a book, or distinguish righteousness from wickedness. She had never heard the Gospel, confessed Jesus as Lord, repented of wrongdoing, or consciously exercised what we mere mortals could readily classify as faith in God. She had likewise never deliberately lied, stolen, worshiped an idol, rejected divine instruction, or committed any other act of accountable rebellion against her Creator that we have prudent grounds to designate. Nevertheless, Daphne was a complete human person, created in the image of God, possessing body, soul, and human spirit. Her brief life did not diminish the value of her personhood or remove her from the knowledge and care of the God who formed her. (Gen 1:26–27; Psa 139:13–16; Zec 12:1; Mat 18:10)

The distinction between inherited corruption and personal guilt is particularly consequential in Daphne's case. Through Adam, sin entered the world, bringing death and corruption upon humanity. Daphne inherited the consequences of that fall, as demonstrated by her mortality, despite the nigh-absolute possibility of having committed no personal sin. Scripture acknowledges those who lack knowledge of good and evil and distinguishes personal guilt from the wrongdoing of another. Daphne had not developed the capacity to understand moral obligations and willfully violate them. (Rom 5:12–14; 1Co 15:21–22; Deu 1:39; Isa 7:15–16; Eze 18:20)

Pardoning of sin, or justification that comes by faith addresses culpable offenses before the court of Heaven. Since Daphne had committed no accountable personal wrongdoing, she requires no pardon for nonexistent offenses. Her original human spirit had likewise never been altered through personal rebellion, removing the particular injury that being "born of water and of spirit" addresses in those who have knowingly sinned. The Holy Spirit remains the giver and sustainer of spiritual life, but there is no necessity to attribute an injury to Daphne's spirit simply to require its restoration. (Rom 4:15; Rom 5:13; Jas 4:17; Job 33:4; Zec 12:1)

Her innocence of personal sin, however, did not eliminate the inherited corruption of the soul. We inherit our human spirit from God breathing afresh to each one of us like He did with Adam in the beginning. That breath contacts the this-world material that we inherit form our parents which is unfortunately inherited unbroken from Adam and bears His corruption. The soul is the chemo-spiritual reaction of teh breath of God upon organic material of this world. Every soul descending form Adam must be cleansed of Adam's corruption. Daphne could not cleanse herself, and her innocence did not independently overcome the consequences of Adam's fall. The cleansing required remains the work of God, accomplished through the blood of Jesus Christ, the Lamb foreknown before the foundation of the world. The redemptive power of Christ is sufficient to address inherited corruption. God gives any grace readily were there is no personal sin. That is the tragic silver lining to Daphne's short life.(Rom 5:15–19; 1Pe 1:18–20; Heb 9:14–15; 1Jo 1:7)

Daphne's inability to exercise conscious faith, cultivate the fear of the Lord through instruction, or receive the revealed Gospel cannot be equated with unbelief, disobedience, or rejection. Those responses presuppose capacities and opportunities she did not possess. God knows the difference between a will hardened against the truth and a child who has not yet developed the capacity to understand it. His judgment considers what the person actually knows, can do, and has done. (Deu 1:39; Isa 7:15–16; Luk 12:47–48; Rom 2:6–16)

The account of David's infant son provides a biblical parallel. After the child died, David declared, "I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me." Jesus likewise welcomed little children, declaring that the Kingdom of God belongs to such as these. These passages place children within the favorable regard of God and provide grounds for understanding Daphne's preservation through divine mercy rather than through accomplishments she was incapable of performing. (2Sa 12:22–23; Mar 10:13–16; Mat 19:14)

The particular inheritance Daphne receives remains subject to the righteous counsel of God. Little Miss Daphne never encountered the circumstances through which believers consciously enter the New Covenant, or receive the promised indwelling of the Holy Spirit, or  undertake the race associated with co-heirship with Christ. Eternal continuance and the specific inheritance of Christ's adopted co-heirs are distinguishable realities. The absence of those earthly experiences does not establish a sentence of destruction upon someone incapable of participating in them. God remains free to provide every blessing compatible with His righteousness, holiness, and eternal purposes. (Rom 8:15–17; Eph 1:13–14; Mat 19:14; Rom 9:15–16; 1Co 2:9)

Daphne therefore illustrates a condition distinct from that of the accountable sinner. She inherited corruption without personally producing guilt, possessed an original human spirit without having willfully corrupted it, and lacked the capacity to perform the acts of faith and obedience ordinarily required of those who receive God's revelation. Upon death her body is left behind, her soul in her spirit return to the gates, her relationship with God unmarred by personal sin is met with the full mercy of God to provide all remaining cleaning or transformation required to bring her home.

Her eternal preservation rests in the same Christ whose blood redeems sinners, whose power overcomes death, and through whom God makes all things new. The saving operations required correspond to her actual condition, while those addressing injuries she never sustained have no corresponding work to perform. (Rom 5:15–21; 1Co 15:21–26; Rev 21:1–5)

Daphne's parents knew her for 366 days. God knew her before the first of them, and her death did not place her beyond His reach. She had done nothing to earn eternal life, and nothing to incur the guilt of personal rebellion. What she could not do for herself remained entirely within the power of the God who formed her, the Lamb who redeemed her, and the Spirit who gives life.

#### The Case of Josephine Johnson: A Hypothetical Person Battered to Bitterness

The most beautiful belle at the ball. Josephine Anastasia Johnson was a darling who wouldn't hurt a fly. Daughter of a substance abuse rehabilitation counselor on one side and a psychiatrist specializing in adults with special needs on the other, Jojo was gently raised with a wealth of wisdom about seeking to understand those she'd meet throughout her life. Everyone has a story and everyone has a voice.

Josephine carried that compassion into adulthood. She was patient with difficult people, generous with her time, and reluctant to hold anyone's worst moment against them. She attended church, volunteered for the city council, and ran marathons for charity. She was successful in the early years of her career and looked forward to raising a family of her own.

One winter evening, after a sleet storm the previous day, shimmering icicles formed on the leafless branches of the trees and lonely gutters running along houses.  Josephine Carefly drove home. You never know when there's hidden ice on the road.  Ms. Jojo approaches her door, pauses just short to pull her keys out of her purse, and when she looks up, the door is ajar... Josephine lives alone. This is terrifying. Maybe, just maybe she left the door open?  She opens the door to find her house ransacked. Any container or drawer is left open, and things are flung everywhere.  Jojo didn't have much more than any other 9-to-5 soul.  She walks through the house, assessing the situation right through the moment when time seems to stand still.

The intruder was still in the house. He heard whoever lived there had come home.  The world may never know what went through this criminal's mind, but he decided to get the drop on Jojo. Whether it was a baton, a pipe, or a mystical staff makes no difference.  The attacker leaped from the shadows and bludgeoned Jojo from head to toe with extreme violence. He struck and stomped on her enough to give her a concussion, crack her pelvis and break several ribs.  Jojo went unconscious. A neighbor found her and called emergency services.

She survived after extensive surgery and months of rehabilitation, but the damage was permanent. She would never walk normally again. Her pelvic injury rendered her unable to bear children. Absolutely devastating.

In almost a miracle of statistics, the criminal was found and apprehended, convicted, and sentenced to life in prison. Josephine attended the sentencing. The court acknowledged the severity of his crimes, and the man who had devastated her life would spend the remainder of his life behind bars.

Josephine was relieved that he could not harm anyone else. But for herself, she refused to forgive him. "If I ever got the chance, I'd still stomp him in the nuts until someone pulled me off," she would say. "He deserves every bit of it."

Her anger was understandable. The man had committed an appalling wrong, and nothing about forgiveness would make his actions less wicked. The Lord does not command. His people call evil good, nor does forgiveness require reconciliation, restored trust, or the abandonment of lawful justice. Scripture permits righteous anger and assigns vengeance to God, who judges every offense perfectly.

But Josephine's anger became something she intended never to release. Years passed, and she continued to insist that the man could rot in prison and beyond for all she cared. She knew Christ's command to forgive and His warning that those who refuse to forgive others will themselves be denied forgiveness. She simply considered her circumstances an exception. Surely God understood what had been done to her. Surely He would not require her to forgive something so terrible.

Josephine continued to do much good. She supported others who had suffered violence, remained generous toward her family and friends, and found ways to serve despite her physical limitations. Yet the injury committed against her had become the foundation of a separate injury she was now committing against herself. The original bitterness had been understandable, but its continued cultivation was willful. She did not simply remember the evil; she maintained a personal claim to vengeance that she refused to surrender to God.

The corruption of her soul did not require her to become cruel to everyone around her. She could love her neighbors, minus one, give generously, and comfort the afflicted while preserving one corner of her heart against the commandment of Christ. A righteous grievance did not sanctify an unrighteous refusal, and the fact that another person had caused her suffering did not make her subsequent choices his responsibility. (Heb 12:14--15; Eph 4:31--32; Col 3:12--13; Jas 2:13)

Josephine lived a long life, bearing both the permanent consequences of the assault and the unforgiveness she had chosen to keep. When she died, she stood before the throne of the Lord, whose knowledge of her life was complete. Every injury, every lost opportunity, every night of pain, and every act of cruelty committed against her was known to Him.

The Lord asked, "Josephine Anastasia Johnson, why did you refuse to forgive the man who harmed you?"

Josephine answered, "Lord, you know what he did to me. He took my health, my ability to have children, and so much of the life I was supposed to have. How could I forgive him?"

The Lord replied, "I knew every blow he struck and every consequence you suffered. His guilt was his own, and my judgment of him is righteous. But I commanded you to forgive, and you would not. You received My mercy while refusing to extend mercy to another. You held to your grievance until your love for vengeance became corruption within you. I did not require you to surrender justice. The Lord's judgment was final.

"For if you forgive others their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you. But if you do not forgive others their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses."

"Bind her hand and foot and cast her into outer darkness."

Josephine had known those words for decades. She had simply believed they could not apply to her.

The violence committed against Josephine was never her fault, and the man responsible never acquired authority over her eternal destiny. Yet neither did his wickedness grant her immunity from the consequences of her own will. She had been grievously injured by another person's sin and subsequently permitted an unforgiving disposition to become a settled part of her own soul.

Her loss was not the result of anger having existed nor of remembering an offense that could never be forgotten. It was the result of the merciless preserving of a corruption that Christ had commanded her to relinquish. The mercy that could have cleansed her was sufficient; the grievance she refused to release was not a lawful exception to holiness.

The Lord who knew every tear Josephine shed also knew every opportunity she had to forgive. His judgment overlooked neither the evil done to her nor the evil she eventually chose to keep. (Mat 6:14--15; Mat 18:32--35; Rom 12:19; Heb 12:15; Gal 6:7--9; Rev 21:7--8, 27)

### Appendix 4:  The Fear of the Lord is to Eschew Evil

The resounding benefit that Jesus brought to the equation with His finished work is that life or perdition as an outcome was severed from the absence of failure, allowing the 100% propagation of effects from Adam to be 100% non-decisive. What remains is to love or hate corruption. Like a principle such as 1+1, gravity or inertia, to love corruption is to become the corruption. The ultimate unfortunate outcome could likely be defeated if the problem were a relationship to corruption or a propensity for corruption, but no, the soul is itself corrupted or returns to corruption willfully. All corruption, whether effect, unbalanced account, or sentient agent, will be cleansed, corrected or cast into perdition. Full stop. If sentient agents could choose corruption without becoming corrupted, they would almost certainly attempt to thread that needle rather than choose the goodness of God. However, as no cognizant alcoholic specifically desires to pickle their liver, and the most perilous even continues upon discovering it, so also is the corruption of the self an unavoidable but decisive danger to all souls and spirits. Could God have created otherwise? Could God have made it so that the decisions of eternal spiritual entities leave the entity's material being unchanged? Perhaps. We can only know what most soundly, validly, cogently or coherently tracks with what has been revealed.
